# Trust, safety and insurance

Whetstone's entire pitch is that you can take advice from a stranger on the
internet without it being a gamble. Here's exactly what stands behind that.

## The Verified badge

Every advisor with a badge has been reviewed **by a person**, not a system. That
review covers:

- **Identity** — they are who they say they are, checked against ID documents
- **Credentials** — professional licences, registrations and memberships are real
  and current
- **Background** — usually a reference call about the experience they describe

Every decision is written down with a note. Nothing is auto-approved and nothing
is auto-rejected — a failed automated check routes to a human, it doesn't end an
application.

**An advisor without that badge cannot appear on a shortlist, cannot be booked
and cannot be paid.** There is no fast lane, including for people with excellent
credentials.

## Insurance

Whetstone holds a **professional indemnity policy** covering the advice given in
sessions booked through the platform.

- You don't buy it, arrange it or opt into it — it's already in place
- The current coverage statement is shown on the booking screen, before you pay
- If coverage were ever not active, **payment would be refused** — the platform
  will not take money for an uninsured session

Advisors: this is coverage you'd otherwise carry yourself, and it's a real part of
what the commission pays for.

## Money held until the work is done

Your payment goes to Whetstone, not the advisor. It's released only when you
confirm the work is complete — or automatically after seven days if you don't
respond and haven't reported a problem.

Reporting a problem stops the money moving. That's the point of the confirmation
step.

## Bounded scope

Every package has a written description of what it covers, and that description
is **copied onto your booking when you book**. If the package changes later, your
booking doesn't. What you agreed to is what you're owed — and, for advisors, what
you're expected to deliver and no more.

## Reporting a problem

Either side can do this, from the booking's message thread or from the completion
confirmation screen. Choose **Report a problem** and describe what happened in
your own words.

What happens then:

1. The booking is marked **Disputed** and **no money moves** — not to the
   advisor, not back to you, until it's resolved.
2. The Whetstone team is alerted immediately. Nobody has to be watching a
   dashboard for it to be seen.
3. A team member reads the booking, the messages, the recorded session outcomes
   and both accounts of what happened.
4. They reach an outcome — commonly a resolution where the engagement is treated
   as complete, or a refund to the client. Complex cases are escalated internally.

Every step is written into the record on that booking.

**Report it rather than absorbing it.** Both sides are better off with a written
record and a person looking at it than with a quiet grievance.

## Good reasons to report

- The session didn't happen and messages went unanswered
- What was delivered doesn't match the scope you booked
- The advice was outside the advisor's stated expertise
- Anyone behaved unprofessionally
- Something in the payment doesn't look right

## Staying safe generally

**Keep it on the platform.** Messages, bookings and payment all live on
Whetstone, and that record is what protects you if there's ever a dispute. An
arrangement moved off-platform has no insurance, no held payment and no evidence
trail.

**Never pay an advisor directly.** No advisor on Whetstone will ask you to. If
one does, report it.

**Advisors: don't take payment outside Whetstone.** It voids your insurance
coverage for that work and breaches the platform terms.

**Share only what's needed.** Real financial context helps; passwords, banking
logins and full identity documents are never needed by an advisor.

**Trust your instincts.** If a session feels wrong, end it and report it. You
won't be penalised for that.

## What advice on Whetstone is and isn't

Advisors share professional judgement built from decades of doing the work. They
are not acting as your accountant, lawyer, doctor or licensed financial adviser
unless they say so explicitly and are registered to do it in your jurisdiction.

Decisions about your business remain yours. Where something is regulated in your
country, take formal advice locally as well — a good Whetstone advisor will tell
you that themselves.
